## Findings

| Claim type | Review finding |
|---|---|
| **Technical — hashes and artifact integrity** | The article correctly explains that identical bytes produce the same digest under the same algorithm. A matching SHA-256 digest provides strong evidence of byte agreement against a trusted reference. The digest alone establishes neither execution history nor scientific validity. NIST’s Secure Hash Standard supports the integrity claim. [NIST FIPS 180-4](https://csrc.nist.gov/files/pubs/fips/180-4/final/docs/fips180-4.pdf) |
| **Methodological — reproduction and independence** | Identical outputs and separate record IDs leave open whether anyone performed a second run. The National Academies defines computational reproducibility in terms of consistent results using the original inputs, code, methods, and analysis conditions. It uses *replicability* for studies that obtain their own data. A separately executed run can reuse the original inputs and code, and consistent results can still share an error or assumption. [National Academies, Reproducibility and Replicability in Science](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25303/chapter/3) |
| **Project history — ARCH-003, DD-001, DD-002, and AR-1.7** | The November 2 freeze (`e6a893173e726c51642827c6972fd291ee262c16`) established four common responsibilities and six governing invariants. DD-001 (`20808ba5a58eb6bf98172750d1caf376ab07f668`), adopted November 2, defines the Research Graph as Records and Relationships and treats Assertion as a specialized Record. DD-002 (`798881cc82f2f27f33601f59b5951fe5128c8879`), adopted November 3, separates durable research identity from content, state, and location and represents lineage through Relationships. The AR-1.7 bounded closure record (`cee28d7a3016223e0f28b776683a780d77fd707e`) records eight open findings. DD-002 confirms all eight remained open with their corrective obligations. [Prior published review of Journal #33](https://github.com/markkoehn0/btlb-feed-monitor/blob/main/reviews/BTLB-scientific-integrity-review-2026-11-08.md) |
| **Author judgment — what a trustworthy framework should preserve** | Keeping claims distinct from evidence, retaining failed or incomplete work, and preserving originals alongside corrections are design choices. W3C PROV-DM provides a precedent for modeling entities, activities, and derivations. Whether ARCH-003 can preserve those distinctions reliably remains an implementation and verification question. [W3C PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/) |
| **Empirical scope** | The article reports conceptual design decisions. It labels Figure 1 and the examples as hypothetical and claims no implemented system, corrective verification, independent reproduction, experiment, or faster-than-light result. The Category 5 label is consistent with that stated scope. Remote reader access to the cited revisions and the Research Method v1.2 classification criteria were not independently checked. |

## Suggested clarifications

1. **Define “independent” in the opening example.** Suggested wording: “Did each researcher execute the computation separately? If both runs used the same data and code, their agreement can support computational reproducibility. Any shared assumptions or software defects remain common to both runs.”
2. **Make the decision history auditable.** Add commit-pinned links or sanitized excerpts for DD-001, DD-002, the freeze, and the AR-1.7 closure record. Cite DD-002 for the status of the eight inherited findings as of November 3.
3. **Explain how a correction becomes visible.** Say that the original remains available for audit and that readers following it should see its superseded status and a link to the correction. This makes the practical consequence of preserving both records clear.
